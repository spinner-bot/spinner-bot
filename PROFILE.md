## English

I am Zhang Ruihan, an undergraduate in Intelligent Science and Technology at Hohai University's School of Artificial Intelligence and Automation, and a member of the HHU Data Intelligence & Governance Lab (DIG Lab) and the 2025 URT undergraduate research team. My primary research interests lie in machine learning and multimodal intelligence, extending into software engineering, with particular attention to the mechanisms by which models acquire, transfer, and retain capabilities, the role of policy distillation in multimodal settings, and the design of tabular learning methods and model architectures. Methodologically, I follow a path of understanding, reproduction, validation, and construction: beginning with a careful reading of the problem, I reproduce existing approaches, then validate, question, and revise them through experimentation before translating conclusions into systems that can actually run. In my view, understanding a paper is not equivalent to mastering it; only by completing an independent implementation and observing whether it succeeds or fails in experiment can one truly engage with the core of the method. This path has unfolded in stages: in the autumn of 2025 I began with programming foundations and initial engineering builds; in the spring of 2026 I started the systematic study of machine learning and participated in research practice; in the summer my focus shifted to multimodal intelligence and policy distillation; and at present I am working more deeply into model architectures while following developments in emerging directions. These stages have led me to recognize that research is not the passive acceptance of established conclusions, but a continuous process of raising questions, designing experiments, and taking responsibility for results. In research practice, I maintain a continuously updated AI repository systematically documenting implementations of and notes on NLP, CV, and frontier methods; I have participated in an AI-assisted hydrodynamic modeling and simulation project; and I have constructed concept systems for examining algorithm validity as well as measurement experiments on AI-based image emotion recognition. This work has fostered a habit of moving iteratively between theoretical understanding and engineering implementation, and has reinforced my attention to the reproducibility and verifiability of research conclusions. Technically, Python is my primary language, and I am familiar with C, C++, and HTML while learning Java; experimental work relies primarily on PyTorch, scikit-learn, OpenCV, and Hugging Face, and daily work is conducted with Git, Docker, Linux, and VS Code. Rather than the tools themselves, I value the shared research abilities behind them: problem decomposition, rapid validation, and continuous iteration. I remain at an early stage of academic training, yet my direction is relatively clear: I aim to conduct solid, verifiable work in machine learning and multimodal intelligence while preserving a full capacity for engineering practice, so that research is not confined to theory; I am also willing to make the research process public on GitHub and to exchange and collaborate with peers working in AI and computer science.
