## English

I'm Zhang Ruihan. I'm an undergraduate in Intelligent Science and Technology at Hohai University's School of Artificial Intelligence and Automation, and a member of the HHU Data Intelligence & Governance Lab (DIG Lab) and the 2025 URT undergraduate research team. My research centers on machine learning and multimodal intelligence, extending into software engineering, with a particular interest in how models acquire, transfer, and retain capabilities, the role of policy distillation in multimodal settings, and the design of tabular learning methods and model architectures; methodologically, I begin by understanding a problem, reproduce existing approaches, and then use experiments to validate, question, and revise them before turning conclusions into systems that actually run—because to me, understanding a paper is not the same as mastering it, and only having implemented it myself and watched it succeed or fail in an experiment makes me feel I have truly come close to it. This path has developed in stages: in the autumn of 2025 I began with programming foundations and my first builds; in the spring of 2026 I started studying machine learning systematically and taking part in research practice; in the summer my focus shifted to multimodal intelligence and policy distillation; and I am now working deeper into model architectures while following emerging directions—each stage reinforcing that research is not the passive absorption of conclusions but the continuous work of asking questions, designing experiments, and being accountable for results. In my research and engineering practice, I maintain a continuously updated AI repository documenting implementations and notes on NLP, CV, and frontier methods, have contributed to an AI-assisted hydrodynamic modeling and simulation project, and have built concept systems for testing whether algorithms hold up as well as measurement experiments around AI-based image emotion recognition, work that has taught me to move between theoretical understanding and engineering implementation and to value conclusions that withstand reproduction and validation. Technically, Python is my primary language, and I am comfortable with C, C++, and HTML while learning Java; I use PyTorch, scikit-learn, OpenCV, and Hugging Face for experiments and work daily with Git, Docker, Linux, and VS Code—yet more than the tools themselves, I value the shared abilities behind them: breaking problems down, validating quickly, and iterating continuously. I am still early in my academic training, but the direction is clear: I want to produce solid, verifiable work in machine learning and multimodal intelligence while keeping the full capacity for engineering practice so that research does not remain on paper, and I am glad to keep this process public on GitHub, exchanging ideas and collaborating with others working in AI and computer science.
