## 中文

我是张锐寒，目前就读于河海大学人工智能与自动化学院，主修智能科学与技术，同时是河海大学数智治域实验室（DIG Lab）和 2025 URT 本科生科研团队的成员。

这条路径由若干具体阶段构成。2025 年秋季，我从编程基础与最初的工程构建起步；2026 年春季，开始系统学习机器学习并参与研究实践；同年夏季，研究重点转向多模态智能与策略蒸馏；目前则深入模型架构，同时关注前沿方向的发展。每个阶段都让我更清楚地认识到，研究不是被动吸收结论，而是持续提出问题、设计实验并为结果负责。

在技术上，Python 是我的主力语言，同时熟悉 C、C++ 和 HTML，正在学习 Java；常用 PyTorch、scikit-learn、OpenCV 与 Hugging Face 进行实验，日常工作基于 Git、Docker、Linux 与 VS Code。相比工具本身，我更看重其背后共通的能力：拆解问题、快速验证与持续迭代。

我仍处在学术训练的早期阶段，但方向是清晰的：希望在机器学习与多模态智能上做出扎实、可验证的工作，同时保持工程实践的完整能力，使研究不止停留在纸面。我相信技术洞察只有经过实验检验、并由真实的系统承载，才会产生真正的价值。我也愿意将研究过程公开在 GitHub 上，与同样从事 AI 与计算机科学研究的人交流、协作。

## English

I'm Zhang Ruihan. I'm an undergraduate in Intelligent Science and Technology at Hohai University's School of Artificial Intelligence and Automation, and a member of the HHU Data Intelligence & Governance Lab (DIG Lab) and the 2025 URT undergraduate research team.

My research centers on machine learning and multimodal intelligence, extending into software engineering. More specifically, I study how models acquire, transfer, and retain capabilities, the role of policy distillation in multimodal settings, and the design of tabular learning methods and model architectures. My approach starts from understanding: read the problem carefully, reproduce existing methods, then use experiments to validate, question, and revise them, and finally turn the conclusions into systems that actually run. To me, understanding a paper is not the same as mastering it—only when I have implemented it myself and observed it succeed or fail in an experiment do I feel I have truly come close to it.

This path has developed in distinct stages. In the autumn of 2025 I began with programming foundations and my first builds; in the spring of 2026 I started studying machine learning systematically and taking part in research practice; in the summer my focus shifted to multimodal intelligence and policy distillation; and I am now working deeper into model architectures while following emerging directions. Each stage has made one thing clearer: research is not the passive absorption of conclusions, but the continuous work of asking questions, designing experiments, and being accountable for the results.

In research and engineering practice, I maintain a continuously updated AI repository documenting implementations and notes on NLP, CV, and frontier methods; I have contributed to an AI-assisted hydrodynamic modeling and simulation project; and I have built concept systems for testing whether algorithms actually hold up, as well as measurement experiments around AI-based image emotion recognition. This work has taught me to move back and forth between theoretical understanding and engineering implementation, and to value conclusions that can withstand reproduction and validation.

Technically, Python is my primary language, and I am comfortable with C, C++, and HTML while learning Java. I use PyTorch, scikit-learn, OpenCV, and Hugging Face for experiments, working daily with Git, Docker, Linux, and VS Code. More than the tools themselves, I value the shared abilities behind them: breaking problems down, validating quickly, and iterating continuously.

I am still early in my academic training, but the direction is clear: I want to produce solid, verifiable work in machine learning and multimodal intelligence, while keeping the full capacity for engineering practice so that research does not remain on paper. I believe technical insight becomes valuable only when it has been tested by experiment and carried by a real system. I am also glad to keep this process public on GitHub, exchanging ideas and collaborating with others working in AI and computer science.
